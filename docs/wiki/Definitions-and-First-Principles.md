# Definitions and First Principles

## Why definitions come first

Two debaters can appear to disagree while using the same word differently. The course therefore requires operational agreement about important terms before argument begins.

Agreement on a definition does **not** imply agreement with the proposition.

## Types of definitions

### Lexical definition
How a word is commonly used.

### Operational definition
How a term will be measured or recognized in a particular investigation.

### Stipulative definition
A definition adopted for the purpose of an argument.

### Theoretical definition
A definition connected to a broader explanatory model.

## Example: limited rendering

Before debating limited rendering, learners should distinguish at least:

**Simulation** — an implemented model whose states evolve according to rules.

**Rendering** — generation or presentation of a representation from underlying state.

**Limited rendering** — for this course, a hypothetical optimization in which some representational detail is generated only when required rather than continuously represented at maximum detail.

**Observation** — acquisition of information through an interaction or measurement process.

**Evidence** — information that raises or lowers rational confidence in a proposition.

**Proof** — a demonstration whose premises and inference establish a conclusion within the relevant system. The course warns students not to use “proof” casually when they mean evidence.

## Definition agreement exercise

Before debate, both sides should answer:

* Do we mean the same thing by the key term?
* Is the definition measurable?
* Does the definition secretly assume the conclusion?
* Would changing the definition change the argument?

This introduces students to equivocation and begging the question before those fallacies are formally named.
