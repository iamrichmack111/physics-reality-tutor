# Debate Methodology

## Two-sided simulation

The course should be capable of assigning a learner either side of a proposition. This separates reasoning skill from personal preference.

## Debate sequence

1. Agree on definitions.
2. State the question.
3. State proposition and counter-proposition.
4. Identify burden of proof.
5. Present three proofs.
6. Express the inferential structure of each proof.
7. Cross-examine premises.
8. Identify possible fallacies.
9. Steelman the opposing case.
10. Give rebuttal.
11. State what evidence would change the conclusion.
12. Restate the final position.

## Burden of proof

The person making a claim carries responsibility for supporting it. Failure to disprove a claim does not automatically establish that claim.

## Steelmanning

A student should summarize the opponent's argument in a form the opponent could accept before rebutting it.

## Scoring dimensions

Debate can be scored separately for:

* definition precision;
* proposition clarity;
* evidence relevance;
* premise quality;
* valid inference;
* counterargument accuracy;
* fallacy recognition;
* steelmanning;
* rebuttal quality;
* epistemic humility;
* final synthesis.

A learner can therefore improve reasoning even when the final conclusion is incorrect.
